What the panels say together.

§1 Demand shows the twenty-year flat line ending. Total generation grew at under 1% a year from 2008 to 2021. It broke out in 2024 and is now compounding above 3%. Data centers alone are projected to consume 600 TWh by 2030 — roughly the entire current generation of France. §2 Supply shows what is being built against that demand. Coal retirements have slowed sharply. Net firm capacity has been falling for a decade. Utility capex is at record highs and rising, but the spending is going into transmission, distribution, and rate base — not new generation capacity.

§3 Bottlenecks shows why. Interconnection queues at 220+ GW. Heavy-duty gas turbine lead times at 4.5 years. Large power transformer lead times at 3+ years. Every input that goes into the grid has roughly doubled since 2019. §4 Prime Movers shows the deeper constraint. Smil's framework: energy transitions are physical device transitions, not just fuel switches. Every prior transition took 50 to 100 years to reach majority share. The capital stock that produces electricity is built to last 30 to 65 years. The transition cannot be willed to happen faster than the underlying machines can be built and replaced. §5 Market Signals shows the financial layer pricing this reality. PJM capacity auction prices jumped 9× in 2025-2026. Residential rates are climbing. Gas is the swing fuel and its price reflects the reality that it backs everything else.

The structural read: the American energy transition is real, ongoing, and constrained — not by policy, not by capital, but by physics, lead times, and the slow turnover of capital stock. Demand is inflecting faster than supply can respond. Prices are starting to reflect what the physical layer has been signaling for years. Pittsburgh built the previous energy system. It knows how long these things actually take.
